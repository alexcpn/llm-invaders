LLM speed experiment · 11 October 2026, 00:23

How fast can a decision model play Space Invaders?

No model came close to arcade speed. Jev, the better of two System-1 decision models, won 1 game in 3 at 10% and at 15% of full speed and none faster; Microsoft-Decision-1 and a local Gemma 4 E2B never won at any speed, because they hardly move the cannon.

26 games · latency charged in game time · total cost $0.213 of a $2.00 budget

Findings

  • Speed caps play even with perfect judgement. A coded player has to decide every 0.115 game seconds to win half its games. At Jev's 0.49 s, Microsoft-Decision-1's 0.58 s and Gemma's 0.21 s per decision, that caps the game at about 23%, 20% and 54% of full speed. Full speed needs a decision in about 0.1 s of real time, roughly five times faster than the fastest System-1 model over the network.
  • Inside that cap, judgement decided the games. On 200 situations from real play, Jev chose a sound move 77% of the time; it lost games by running out of lives, dodging 36 of 50 bombs. Microsoft-Decision-1 chose well 28% of the time: it waited in 132 of 200 situations and never dodged. Gemma 4 E2B fired in all 200, so it only hit aliens that passed over it, 12 of 18 in every game.
  • It was cheap. 14,238 model decisions over 26 games, plus about 700 quality and latency questions, cost $0.21 in total ($0.17 Jev, $0.04 Microsoft-Decision-1, local Gemma free): about $0.00002 per Jev decision and $0.000007 per Decision-1 decision. Cost is not what stops decision models playing in real time; latency and judgement are.

How it was measured

The game is a small, deterministic Space Invaders: 18 aliens march sideways, drop a row at each edge, speed up as they thin out and drop bombs. The cannon moves one column per action and has one shot in flight. Clearing the wave wins; the aliens landing or losing three lives loses.

Each decision, the game freezes, the model gets the board as a short text description and picks one action: left, right, fire or wait. The game then runs on for the model's measured latency times the game speed before the action lands, so a slow model acts on a stale view, as it would in real play, while results stay reproducible. This follows the latency-controlled protocol used by OmniGameArena.

Jev and Microsoft-Decision-1 are System-1 decision models: they return a probability for each fixed option in one pass instead of writing text, called through OpenRouter's /api/alpha/decisions endpoint; the most likely action is played. Gemma 4 E2B runs on the laptop's RTX 3060 through Ollama with thinking switched off and a strict JSON answer. Every paid call was logged against a hard $1.90 stop.
